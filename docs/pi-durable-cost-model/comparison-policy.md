# Comparison policy fixed before the next candidate measurements

No claim of Pi-comparable performance based on a favorable case or wall time alone. The operational non-inferiority threshold will be an upper one-sided 95% confidence limit of 1.10 for paired rustX/Pi cost ratios, in each of all six cases. Better-than-Pi cost is allowed. Metrics: measured-turn wall, measured-turn self CPU, full lifecycle self CPU, lifecycle process-attributed writes, and maximum sampled anonymous PSS. Also report lifecycle wall, provider/controller/startup overhead, sampled total PSS/RSS, persistence footprints, CPU resolution, and phase costs; these remain visible even when outside the gate. This is a declared engineering criterion, not a user-approved weakening of any product contract.

Use at least seven clean, interleaved pairs per case for a final parity claim (three pairs suffice for each exploratory revision comparison). Retain failed and slow samples; any failed ordered oracle blocks acceptance. Comparison must include full expected/wire/captured/frozen/reopened requests and results and negative oracle tests. Do not subtract unrelated medians. Individual ratios, arithmetic costs, raw samples and uncertainty stay available.

For positive metrics report paired geometric mean and Student-t interval on log ratios (seven pairs: df 6, one-sided 95% critical 1.94318), as well as medians and complete observed range. This interval assumes approximately normal independent log ratios and is limited by a small cloud sample; no universal performance guarantee. Zero or unavailable CPU denominators produce an indeterminate ratio, never a favorable infinity/zero replacement. Report /proc tick resolution and its magnitude relative to each duration. A final comparable claim cannot pass an indeterminate metric; increase independently repeated workload observations or report the unresolved resolution limit. Sampled memory is not peak memory. Allocations are unavailable unless separately measured with a documented profiler.

Formal timings run without builds, profilers, compression or other benchmark processes. Same fixed optimized Rust settings and pinned Pi/Node runtime; new processes and stores. External controller owns sibling product/provider; provider remains alive through product cleanup. Verification and evidence-file output phases remain separately attributable. Source/canonical marginal costs remain separate from unavoidable context construction. Diagnostic SQL/Source/WAL runs are not formal samples, and their instrumented times do not enter the intervals.

## Observation modes

Final cross-engine comparisons must identify and separately report two aligned modes in raw sample identity and summaries:

- `no-live-subscriber`: neither engine maintains a live display subscription during measured turns. Both still verify authoritative settlement, complete ordered requests/results, a late snapshot, historical reads, close/reopen and cleanup. Late snapshot/index/replay costs remain visible in their own phases and the complete lifecycle.
- `live-subscriber`: each engine's actual production observation adapter delivers useful current conversation updates to a real consumer. Pi uses its SQLite Harness ConversationViews/watchEvents surface, not the separate JSONL coding-agent TUI. rustX uses its production App notification surface. Consumers validate ordered updates and final state, including streaming/tool/cancellation results; native payload/schema and carrier differences remain explicit. No synthetic delay or useless work is added to Pi.

The existing `native-workflow` series remains a separately labeled historical comparison: Pi settles submissions without mounting ConversationViews, whereas rustX consumes App notifications. It must not be relabeled as either aligned mode. The seven-pair criterion applies separately to every case and cost metric in each aligned mode; pooling modes cannot establish comparability. Workload, observer mode, runtime/revision, provider protocol/chunk schedule and verification scope are part of the pairing identity. Invalid or missing mode identity is rejected. These are predeclared requirements before the new mode measurements, not claims that their harness implementation or validation is complete.
